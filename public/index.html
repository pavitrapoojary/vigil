Hours saved = cases × auto-routed share × (manual minutes − spot-check minutes) ÷ 60. The minutes are assumptions, not measurements. Missed serious cases at this threshold are shown above so the saving is never read without its risk.

Control design

  • Human in the loop: every output is a recommendation. Serious cases and all rule hits are confirmed by a medical reviewer.
  • Safe-fail routing: low confidence, rule hits, or a more-serious second opinion all send the case to manual review.
  • Validation (GxP): intended-use statement, URS, risk assessment, frozen test set, pre-agreed acceptance criteria (for example serious-case recall), documented IQ/OQ/PQ-style evidence, change control on every retrain.
  • Audit trail: input hash, model version, threshold, probabilities, rules fired, routing, reviewer action, timestamp (ALCOA+).
  • Drift monitoring: monthly serious-share, confidence distribution, override rate and sampled QC re-review; retrain triggers defined in advance.
  • Data protection: strip identifiers before any external LLM call; use a contracted enterprise endpoint in production.

Risks & change management

  • Reviewer trust: show reasons and flagged terms; publish error analysis; start in shadow mode, with AI output hidden, and compare.
  • Automation bias: reviewers may rubber-stamp. Mitigate with random blind QC samples and override-rate tracking.
  • Regulator expectations: clock and quality rules still apply; document the model as a decision-support tool under the PV quality system.
  • Accountability: the Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance and the Head of Safety stay accountable; the model owner is accountable for performance and monitoring.
  • Rollout: shadow mode, then assisted triage for non-serious auto-routing only, then expand if metrics hold.

Method

  • Data: VAERS 2025 DATA and SYMPTOMS files, joined on VAERS_ID. Repeated IDs (follow-up versions) merged: flags OR-ed, longest narrative kept.
  • Labels: P1 = died or life-threatening; P2 = hospitalised or disabled; P3 = everything else. Taken from the reporter's own flags.
  • No leakage: the flags and structured fields are removed from model input. Only the narrative text is used.
  • Baseline model: TF-IDF (1-2 grams) + class-weighted logistic regression, 70/15/15 stratified split; C chosen on validation for serious-case recall; reported on the untouched test set.
  • Rules layer: regex triggers for events of special interest and death/arrest, with a simple negation check. Rules can only raise priority, never lower it.
  • Second opinion: Gemini few-shot prompt, advisory only. Measured with ml/eval_gemini.py.

Honest caveats

  • VAERS is a spontaneous reporting system: noisy, unverified, with duplicates.
  • Labels reflect what the reporter ticked, not clinical adjudication. P2 in particular is inconsistent: many narratives of hospital stays have no flag.
  • Narratives often state the outcome ("died", "hospitalised"), so part of the performance is reading obvious words, not clinical inference.
  • Random split, one year of data, one vaccine-focused system. No external validation and no temporal hold-out.
  • Rule negation handling is crude: family history ("father died") can fire the death rule. That is why rule hits go to a human.
  • This is a prototype, not a validated system.
